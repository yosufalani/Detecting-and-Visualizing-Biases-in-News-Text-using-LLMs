# VeriBias Prototype Feedback Study

## Purpose

This document summarizes a short exploratory feedback study of the VeriBias prototype. The purpose was to collect early user feedback on whether the interface helped users inspect possible bias in news articles.

This was not a formal usability experiment. It was a brief prototype feedback session intended to identify strengths, weaknesses, and possible improvements.

## Participants

A total of 15 students participated. No names were collected. Participants are referred to anonymously as Student A to Student O.

The participants had the following academic backgrounds:

| Study background | Number of participants |
|---|---:|
| Computer engineering | 8 |
| Electrical engineering | 3 |
| Civil engineering | 2 |
| Mechanical engineering | 1 |
| Teacher education | 1 |
| **Total** | **15** |

Most participants were first- or second-year students. The teacher education student was a fifth-year student completing a master's degree.

Most participants had limited prior knowledge of bias in news text. Several understood the general idea that news can be one-sided or emotionally worded, but they found the specific bias categories more difficult to distinguish. The teacher education student was the only participant who had clearly heard about media bias as a concept before the study.

## Procedure

Each participant received a short explanation of the purpose of VeriBias. The participant then used the prototype for approximately five minutes. After using the tool, the participant answered a short set of feedback questions.

The questions were:

1. Did the highlighted text spans help you understand possible bias?
2. Were the explanations clear?
3. Did the confidence score make you trust the model more?
4. Did comparing Gemini and Claude help?
5. Would you personally use this tool for critical reading?
6. Do you think other people could use this tool for critical reading?
7. What did you think of the interface design?
8. What overall score would you give the prototype from 1 to 10?

## Summary of Feedback

| Question | Result | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Did the highlighted text spans help you understand possible bias? | Yes, 15/15 | Participants found the highlights useful because they connected the model output directly to the article text. |
| Were the explanations clear? | Mostly yes, but mixed | Several participants said the explanations could be simpler. |
| Did the confidence score make you trust the model more? | Yes, 15/15 | Confidence increased trust, but this also shows a possible risk of over-trust. |
| Did comparing Gemini and Claude help? | Generally yes | Participants found it useful to see whether the two models agreed or disagreed. |
| Would you personally use this tool for critical reading? | Yes, 4/15 | Only a minority said they would personally use it regularly. |
| Do you think other people could use this tool for critical reading? | Yes, 15/15 | All participants believed the tool could be useful for other readers. |

## Design Feedback

Feedback on the visual design was mixed. Some participants liked the minimalist style because it made the interface look clean and focused. Others suggested using more color to separate bias categories more clearly. One participant suggested adding a dark mode.
